Edge AI, digital twins, autonomous driving

Where should a self-driving car think?

On the car, the brain is fast but small. In the cloud, it's smarter but has to cross the mobile network. I ran 360 simulated drives through dense traffic over 5G, busy 4G, and a weak signal to see when each choice keeps the car safe.

Why the network matters

Round-trip times for each network. The cloud needs about to think, so answers must come back within to meet the 150 ms deadline.

How often should the car ask?

Adaptive switcher on 5G: it asks the cloud only when the edge model is unsure. Stricter gates ask more and crash less.

How it works

Two brains, one deadline

Five decisions per second, each with a hard 150 ms deadline. At the deadline the car acts on whatever answer it has.

Engineering

What went wrong, and what I learned

Most of this project was finding out why the first version gave misleading answers.

PROBLEM 1, THE SIMULATOR

The car couldn't brake

By default the controlled car can't drop below 20 m/s while traffic drives at about 21, so crashes were often unavoidable no matter how smart the decision.

Fix: allow speeds down to 10 m/s. Planner crashes fell from 9 of 24 drives to 2.

PROBLEM 2, SPEED

The digital twin was too slow

Copying the whole simulator for each look-ahead took 170 ms, longer than a decision is allowed.

Fix: twin only the traffic within 70 m and step the physics directly: 12× faster.

PROBLEM 3, THE EDGE MODEL

It learned "just drive fast"

The first small model crashed in all 12 test drives. Bigger models didn't help; five designs all barely beat driving straight. It couldn't see the danger: nothing told it the car was mid-lane-change or that someone was cutting in.

Fix: add those signals plus time-to-collision, learn the planner's scores, not just its choice, and use DAgger. Result: 9 crashes in 24 drives instead of 24.

PROBLEM 4, MY OWN SWITCHER

It was too cautious to help

My first switcher asked the cloud only when an answer was almost certain to arrive in time. On busy 4G it never asked, so it did no better than having no cloud.

Fix: with a backup in place, a late answer only costs bandwidth, so ask whenever there's a fair (25%+) chance. Crashes on 4G fell from 7 to 2.

Honest limits

  • The three network profiles are modeling assumptions for illustration, not measurements. edgecloud measure records your real connection and fits a profile from it.
  • 30 drives per cell: big gaps (24 vs 6 crashes) are clear; small ones (2 vs 4) are within noise.
  • Cloud compute time was measured on one laptop-class CPU. A real server would think faster, which helps cloud strategies.
  • highway-env is a simplified simulator, good for comparing strategies, not for certifying a vehicle.
